Traditional Japanese Shiatsu

Shiatsu works with the body's own energy — applying gentle, sustained pressure to support balance, ease tension and restore a sense of calm.

01

What is Shiatsu?

Shiatsu is a form of Japanese bodywork rooted in the principles of traditional Chinese medicine. The word means "finger pressure" — though palms, elbows and knees are also used. The practice works along the body's meridians, or energy pathways, applying sustained pressure to points where energy has become blocked or depleted.

Unlike many massage styles, Shiatsu is typically received fully clothed, on a floor mat. Sessions are slow, quiet and attentive — shaped by what the body communicates rather than a fixed routine.

02

What to expect

A session usually begins with a short conversation about how you're feeling — physically and otherwise. From there, the work is guided by what I find in the body: areas of tension, restriction or depletion that suggest where attention is needed.

Most people experience a deep sense of relaxation during and after a session. Some feel immediate relief from pain or stiffness. Others notice a gradual shift over the following days.

03

Who it may help

Shiatsu is suitable for most people. It is often sought for musculoskeletal pain, stress, fatigue, sleep difficulties and digestive issues — but its effects are rarely confined to a single complaint. Treating the whole person tends to bring about shifts that a symptom-focused approach may miss.

If you are unsure whether Shiatsu is appropriate for your situation, please get in touch. I am happy to talk it through before you commit to a session.

04

Combined therapies

Depending on what your body needs, a session may also draw on cupping, dry needling, moxibustion, energy medicine, exercise therapy or guidance on food as medicine. These are not added for variety — each is chosen deliberately, when it is the most appropriate tool for what we are working with.
